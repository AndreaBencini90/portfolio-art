The Synchronism of Angles in Attraction

2025 · Print on canvas · 120×120 cm

This graph doesn't show atoms. It shows angles — the hinges along a molecule's backbone, the points where the chain folds. Not the sculpture of the molecule: its choreography. Each edge isn't a chemical bond, but a statistical dependency: when one hinge turns, which others turn with it?

Here the question is asked of the Attraction phase: CRF, the butterfly in the stomach, the thin line between anxiety and excitement; Met-Enkephalin PC, the pleasure of contact; Met-Enkephalin Bic, the same molecule in a different context — the shape that depends on where you are. The synchronism is the question of whether, and how much, these three actors truly move together.

The answer doesn't carry the same degree of certainty everywhere, and this is stated, not hidden. For Met-Enkephalin, 80 real NMR conformers exist: the synchronism of its angles is measured. For CRF, the angles far outnumber the available data — the correlation between its hinges is estimated from the structure, with an honest margin of noise, where the real data isn't enough.

Attraction is when several molecular actors begin to move to the same beat — not because they're chemically bonded, but because they respond to the same signal. The graph tries to show exactly this: not the matter of attraction, but its timing.
