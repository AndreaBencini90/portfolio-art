Method

Every work starts from real data, not a prompt. The path is always the same, across both cycles: measured data becomes a graph, the graph a statistical estimate, the estimate an image. Along that path there are choices — and that's where the author lives.

The technical detail, collection by collection

The method of Emotion in Matter Loop SpaceTime · coming soon
01 — Origin

The real data

The starting point is a measured structure, not an imagined one. For Emotion in Matter these are real molecular structures deposited in the Protein Data Bank (atomic coordinates, often from nuclear magnetic resonance). For Loop SpaceTime, models from theoretical physics in which spacetime emerges from discrete relations.

02 — Structure

The algorithm: from data to graph

The data is translated into a graph: nodes are the elements (atoms, residues, points in space), edges their relations of proximity or interaction. What matters isn't the isolated object, but the web of connections. Transformations are applied to this network — such as the Fourier transform on the graph — that carry the signal into the domain of its relations.

03 — Inference

The estimate: reconstructing relations from the data

Through statistical models (regularised covariance, GraphicalLasso) it's estimated which connections are direct — which pairs stay linked even when accounting for all the others. This is the phase where one tries to reconstruct, from the data, the map of what holds the system together.

04 — Authorship

The discrepancy: my part

Between the data and the final image a discrepancy remains: the choice of starting structure, of parameters, of random seed; the layout, the palette, the volume and silence of each element. This discrepancy isn't an error to eliminate: it's the author's decision. It is irreducible, and recognisable.

This is where it differs from an image generated by a prompt. The process is anchored to real data and reproducible only up to a point — then the choosing begins. The work is the trace of that choice within a larger system.
